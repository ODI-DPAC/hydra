The Smithsonian Institution High Performance Cluster (SI/HPC), informally known as "Hydra" is the primary high performance computing resource at the Smithsonian Institution.


- It is housed at the Ashburn Data Center, VA and is supported by ODI/CDAIO/DPAC.
- Hydra is a Linux distributed cluster consisting of
    - some 5,900 CPU cores,
    - distributed over ~80 compute nodes,
    - totaling over 43TB of total RAM,
    - all interconnected using 10Gbps Ethernet and 100Gps InfiniBand fabrics
- Some 4PB of storage is available using a 3-tier architecture:
    - high availability disk space (NetApp, via NFS),
    - high performance disk space (GPFS, via IB), and
    - near-line low cost disk space (NAS/ZFS, via NFS), only on a subset of nodes.


## **Requesting an Account on Hydra**


- Accounts are requested via OCIO's [Accounts & Access Service Now Catalog](https://smithsonianprod.servicenowservices.com/si?id=sc_category&catalog_id=9ff217131b531c1078932f41f54bcbf2),
    - select the [HPC Account Request](https://smithsonianprod.servicenowservices.com/si?id=sc_cat_item&sys_id=962e05331b96e05078932f41f54bcb3b&sysparm_category=8b5b9d421b601410520ba82eac4bcb65&catalog_id=9ff217131b531c1078932f41f54bcbf2),
    - As of Jun 2026, this applies to all SI's users, i.e., including SAO/CfA's affiliates.


## **Citing Hydra and its DOI**


If you have used Hydra for work that will be a part of a scholarly or professional publication, please cite it.


**Here is a citation example:**


"*Smithsonian Institution High Performance Computing Cluster. Smithsonian Institution. [https://doi.org/10.25572/SIHPC](https://doi.org/10.25572/SIHPC)*"


**or as an acknowledgment:**


"*(Some of) [Tt]he computations in this paper were conducted on the Smithsonian High Performance Cluster (SI/HPC), Smithsonian Institution. [https://doi.org/10.25572/SIHPC](https://doi.org/10.25572/SIHPC)*".


We also would very much appreciate being notified of such citations, so please email us ([SI-HPC\@si.edu](mailto:SIHPC@si.edu)) the reference to the citation, and if you can think of one, add a nice illustration with a short caption in layperson's terms.
